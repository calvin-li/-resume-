Situation: 
Post-merger, Zonar needed to quickly build a new, combined platform to show value to our customers. This was a company-wide effort, and the Data Services team was responsible for the driver performance portion. We would get safety events from vendor we were already working with, but we had to build a new pipeline with enrichment steps, video integration, and better scaling.

Task: 
I had three tasks on this project. One, build the Kafka ingestion service. The existing ingestion used webhooks and cloud function, and we were worried it wouldn't scale as we added more devices. Two, help with the enrichment and data writer services. Contribute to the design, make sure it works with the ingestion service, answer question from the team about the vendor, and know it well enough to handle on-call incidents. Three, lead the Vietnam contractor team as they build the video integration service.

Action: 
My first focus was the ingestion system. Ultimately, we weren't trying to do anything fancy at the ignestion step, just filter for the message types we want, and get them into our cloud. We had just started using Copilot as a company at the time, and it was a big help in getting the Kafka boilerplate set up. I also made sure to confirm the message format with the vendor early on, and settle on an output message format with the team. That way the downstream services can proceed without real data. For the enrichment and writer services, I left the coding to my team mates, and participated in the design meetings and code reviews. This is standard M.O. for the team and keeps everyone informed about all the services they don't directly work on. For the Vietnam team, we wrote the JIRA epics and tickets needed for the services, and placed it on their timeline. The team self-assigns the the tickets and manage their own deployments and code reviews. I stay informed on the progress of their tickets through JIRA and weekly syncs, update the tickets and timelines as necessary when requirements evolve.

Result:
Leadership had set out a six week timeline to get everything dev complete, and we got the driver performance portion demo-ready by then. I managed to get the Kafka service ready fairly early, since it was simple. Maybe 2-3 weeks in, it was deployed to production. The enrichment and writer services had more dependencies and contributors, so they took a little longer but it gave me more time to learn how they worked. The video integration service finished last, but the requirements also took the longest to nail down, and there were some Vietnam holidays near the deadline. I stepped in, leveraged Copilot to set up Apigee and write the production terraform files, and deployed in front of the deadline. There was some followup work after the demo deadline, and a few weeks later the service was production ready and is still in use today.
