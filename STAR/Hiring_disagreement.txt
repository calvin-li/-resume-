Situation:
A few years ago, my team was trying to hire some software engineers. Our interview process was a take-home project followed by a face-to-face interview with the team. I was responsible for setting up the take-home project in addition to participating in the interviews themselves. We were having trouble finding candidates that even made it past the take-home stage, and my manager at the time wanted to lower the requirements or ease up the rubric, especially for the take-home. I felt like that would result in us dedicating time to interview candidates that had no hope of passing the interview, or worse yet, hire someone inadequate for the role.

Task:
I had to sit down with my manager and work out our differences. It was especially important for me that we didn't lower our standards for the final decision. I didn't want management overriding a no-hire from the team.

Action:
I made my case that a new team member who was a poor fit would be a negative for the overall team productivity. My manager felt that we were presenting ourselves like big tech when it came to interview difficulty. Ultimately, we compromised on giving any candidates that completed the take-home a final interview, while keeping the final rubric as-is. Then we'd evaluate the results of both steps and make a decision.

Result:
This shortened the interview process. I managed to not lower our hiring standards while crucially, also not damaging my relationship with my manager. It helped a lot that I listened to their point of view and agreed to some changes to our process. Those changes *could* in theory find a good candidate that just did poorly on the first stage. Ultimately, we expanded our search radius and looked outside of Seattle, while also interviewing coding bootcamp graduates. This led us very quickly to three good candidates, all of whom would have passed the original interview. We extended offers to all three and they all accepted. The entire series of events was a great learning experience for me, regardless of how long the process took.
