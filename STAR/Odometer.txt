Situation:
- Shortly after I started at Zonar we were developing a "Light Duty" product. 
- It  was meant to provide most of the tracking features of our regular hardware, but at a lower cost and with easier installation.
- We had to replicate the features of the in-market product as much as possible
- We were working with a new hardware vendor and making the firmware in-house
- The device only got odometer data when the vehicle was stopped.
- The discrepancy will grow large over the course of a highway trip

Task:
-  I had to build a system to estimate the odometer while the vehicle was moving.
- The estimation needed to be accurate, but err on the side of underestimation. Our frontend did not support odometer numbers decreasing

Action:
- My research found two ways of doing the estimation
- Use the current and previous GPS points
- Average the speeds from the previous two points, and multiply by time between the two points
- I built the odometer function to keep track of all the estimation methods (GPS, speed, etc), correcting them whenever we got a real odometer reading. The function was coded to make it easy to change the order of preference of the methods.
- I also added metrics to track the difference between each estimation method and real odometer.
- This gave us insight over time into which method provided the most high-quality estimates.
- Finally, I set up field testing with our customer care team
- We had several Zonar employees install devices to their own cars and also had someone rent vehicles and try driving them with various routes and connectivity scenarios. 

Result:
- Initially, we had some trouble getting data good enough to actually use for estimates.
- The big problem was that data could arrive out of order, missing data due to connection issues, and semantic differences in the device and our backend (null island issue).
- We stuck with our field testing and incorporated data from customers that saw problems.
- I added an ordering function that could predict if GPS points were missing for a device and hold its data until the missing points arrived. The data didn't have sequence numbers, but I was able to use the timestamp to guess if there was missing data
- This brought our in-order rate from the low 90s up to 99.9%.
- We also developed more safeguards against obvious odometer over-estimates due to bad data, preferring to show no information over bad information.
- By working diligently and being responsive to customers and field testers, my system was able to get the odometer estimate consistently within 1% of the real reading
- Most importantly, we made out beta customers happy
