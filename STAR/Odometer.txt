Situation:
Shortly after I started at Zonar we were developing a "Light Duty" product. It  was meant to provide most of the tracking features of our regular hardware, but at a lower cost and with easier installation. One of the compromises we had to make was that the device only got odometer data when the vehicle was stopped. Therefore, we had to estimate the odometer while the vehicle was moving.

Task:
We identified several potential estimation methods. I was responsible for adding them to the data enrichment function. Since this was a completely new type of product for us, we weren't sure which method would work the best, so my implementation needed to be flexible. Furthermore, the odometer estimate can never decrease and we needed to handle all the various corner cases that can happen.

Action:
I built the odometer function to keep track of all the estimation methods (GPS, speed, etc), correcting them whenever we got a real odometer reading. The function was coded to make it easy to change the order of preference of the methods. Furthermore, I also added metrics to track the difference between each estimation method and real odometer. This gave us insight over time into which method provided the most high-quality estimates. Finally, I worked with our customer care team to do field testing. We had several Zonar employees install devices to their own cars and also had someone rent vehicles and try driving them with various routes and connectivity scenarios. 

Result:
Initially, we had some trouble getting data good enough to actually use for estimates. The big problem was that data could arrive out of order, missing data due to connection issues, and semantic differences in the device and our backend (null island issue). We stuck with our field testing and incorporated data from customers that saw problems. Along the way, we build more robustness into the estimation system. For example, I added an ordering function that could predict if GPS points were missing for a device and hold its data until the missing points arrived. This brought our in-order rate from the low 90s up to 99.9%. We also developed more safeguards against nonsensical odometer estimates due to bad data, preferring to show no information over bad information. By working diligently and being responsive to customers and field testers, my system was able to get the odometer estimate consistently within 1% of the real reading
